# Publication-Ready Figure Legends (SCI Manuscript)

**Figure 1 | PRISMA 2020 flow diagram of literature identification, screening, eligibility assessment, and four-dimensional standardization.**
A total of 456 English-language records (articles and reviews) published between January 1, 2000, and October 31, 2026, were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection (SCIE). After excluding 3 retracted publications ($n = 453$ retained in the full standardized dataset) and 91 off-topic records (46 pure cystic echinococcosis or non-echinococcal records matched only via Keywords Plus, 31 veterinary or preclinical animal/in-vitro studies, and 14 exclusively extrahepatic primary cases), 362 core clinical publications (304 original research articles and 58 clinical reviews) focusing on the surgical and multidisciplinary management of hepatic alveolar echinococcosis (HAE) were included in the final bibliometric analysis.

**Figure 2 | Global annual publication output, three-stage evolutionary trajectory, and citation impact in HAE surgical research from 2000 to October 31, 2026.**
**a**, Annual number of publications (bars, left axis) and cumulative publication growth curve (teal line, right axis) across three distinct clinical evolutionary phases: Phase I (2000–2009, exploratory and PNM staging standardization era, mean 6.0 papers/year), Phase II (2010–2018, consensus consolidation and ex vivo liver resection and autotransplantation [ELRA] pioneering era, mean 11.4 papers/year), and Phase III (2019–2026, rapid expansion of radical hepatectomy, vascular reconstruction, and precision surgery, mean 24.8 papers/year). **b**, Annual total citations received (light blue bars, left axis) and mean citations per paper (CPP, orange line, right axis), highlighting three landmark citation peaks in 2003 (European collaborative liver transplantation report, 772 citations), 2010 (WHO-IWGE expert consensus, 1,955 citations), and 2019 (*Clinical Microbiology Reviews* landmark synthesis and ELRA expansion, 1,145 citations). Source data are provided in `fig2_annual_trends.csv`.

**Figure 3 | Geographic and institutional distribution and international collaboration networks in HAE surgical research ($n = 362$).**
**a**, Top 10 most productive countries/regions ranked by publication volume, annotated with total publications and mean citations per paper (CPP). **b**, Country co-authorship network among the top 12 productive countries; node size is proportional to publication output, edge thickness reflects collaboration frequency, and node colors denote regional clusters (orange: China; teal: European endemic/research core; amber: North America, Middle East, and East Asia). **c**, Top 10 most productive institutions after affiliative unification (orange: Chinese clinical centers; teal: European academic centers). **d**, Institutional collaboration network illustrating the dual-core structure anchored by Western Chinese high-volume surgical centers (Xinjiang Medical University, Sichuan University West China Hospital, and Qinghai University) and European reference centers (University of Franche-Comté/CHU Besançon, Ulm University, and University of Bern). Source data are provided in `fig3_countries_institutions.csv`.

**Figure 4 | Prolific authors, longitudinal scholarly output trajectories, core journals, and Bradford's law distribution ($n = 362$).**
**a**, Co-authorship network of the top 12 disambiguated authors, highlighting three major collaborative clusters: the Xinjiang Medical University ELRA and vascular surgery team (orange: Aji T, Wen H, Shao YM, Jiang TM), the West China Hospital and Qinghai hepatobiliary surgery teams (amber: Wang WT, Yang XW, Fan HN), and the European multidisciplinary network (teal: Vuitton DA, Bresson-Hadni S, Gottstein B, Gruener B, Kern P). **b**, Temporal publication and citation trajectories of the top 10 prolific authors from 2000 to 2026; circle size indicates annual publication count and circle color reflects annual citation impact (orange: $\ge 50$ citations; medium blue: $15–49$ citations; light blue: $<15$ citations). **c**, Top 10 core journals ranked by publication count and annotated with CPP (orange bars indicate high-impact journals with $\text{CPP} \ge 30$). **d**, Bradford's law of scattering curve across all 170 journals on a logarithmic rank scale, partitioning sources into Zone 1 (Core zone: 17 journals contributing 123 papers), Zone 2 (Middle zone: 46 journals), and Zone 3 (Peripheral zone: 107 journals). Source data are provided in `fig4_authors_journals.csv`.

**Figure 5 | Intellectual foundation and co-citation analysis of landmark references in HAE surgical management ($n = 362$).**
**a**, Co-citation network of the top 12 most frequently co-cited references; node size reflects co-citation frequency, edge width represents co-citation strength within the same bibliography, and node color denotes intellectual clusters (teal: WHO-IWGE guidelines and PNM classification; orange: surgical resection, allogeneic liver transplantation, and ELRA clinical cohorts; blue: natural history, epidemiology, and antiparasitic chemotherapy). **b**, Ranking of the top 12 milestone co-cited references by co-citation frequency within the core dataset. Source data are provided in `fig5_cocitation_landmarks.csv`.

**Figure 6 | Keyword co-occurrence network, temporal evolution of average publication year, cluster trajectories, and thematic strategic diagram ($n = 362$).**
**a**, Co-occurrence network of the 12 core clinical and surgical keywords (combined standardized Author Keywords and Keywords Plus), colored by four major thematic clusters. **b**, Temporal evolution of 16 representative surgical and clinical terms ranked by their average publication year (APY), demonstrating a clear paradigm shift from early medical therapy and metabolic imaging (`antiparasitic therapy`, $\text{APY} = 2011.6$; `albendazole`, $\text{APY} = 2013.0$; `PET/CT`, $\text{APY} = 2015.4$) toward complex radical surgery (`hepatectomy`, $\text{APY} = 2020.7$; `ELRA`, $\text{APY} = 2021.0$; `vascular reconstruction`, $\text{APY} = 2021.5$). **c**, Publication trajectory of the four major thematic clusters across the three historical eras (2000–2009, 2010–2018, and 2019–2026). **d**, Callon thematic strategic diagram plotting cluster centrality (relevance degree) against density (development degree) across four quadrants: Motor themes (upper right: Radical Hepatectomy and ELRA/IVC Reconstruction), Basic/Transversal themes (lower right: PNM Staging/Albendazole and Allogeneic LTx), Niche themes (upper left: PET/CT Viability), and Emerging/Declining themes (lower left: 3D Reconstruction, ICG Fluorescence, and Laparoscopy). Source data are provided in `fig6_keywords_clusters.csv`.

**Figure 7 | Top 20 keywords with the strongest citation bursts and five-year evolution of surgical and diagnostic modalities (2000–2026).**
**a**, Chronological distribution of the top 20 burst keywords detected via log-likelihood ratio (LLR) burst analysis from 2000 to October 31, 2026. Light blue bars denote the 27-year baseline period, crimson segments mark the active burst duration (`Begin–End`), and right-hand labels report LLR burst strength and active years. **b**, Heatmap of publication frequency across eight core surgical and diagnostic modalities over five sequential periods (2000–2005, 2006–2010, 2011–2015, 2016–2020, and 2021–2026), highlighting the rapid rise of radical hepatectomy, ELRA/autotransplantation, and retrohepatic inferior vena cava (IVC) reconstruction in the last decade. Source data are provided in `fig7_keyword_bursts.csv`.
